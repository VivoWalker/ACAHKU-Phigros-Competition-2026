# ACAHKU Phigros Competition: competition rules

These English rules reflect the Phigros competition proposal for Session 2026–2027. The source and the operational interpretations required where it is incomplete are recorded in [the proposal rule audit](proposal-rule-audit.md). ACA retains final authority over disputes and cases not covered by the rules.

## Participation and equipment

- Competitors may be ACA members, HKU students or staff, or members of the public. The event accepts up to 30 registered competitors.
- Each competitor must bring their own phone or tablet with the global, legitimate version of Phigros installed, and their own headphones. Cracked or unlicensed versions are not allowed.
- Competitors must use headphones during play. The organisers do not supply spare headphones; a competitor unable to obtain headphones before the competition starts is disqualified.
- Competitors may bring gloves, finger protection, non-slip mats and similar accessories. ACA does not supply these accessories. Arbiters may inspect them to ensure fair play.
- Competitors should unlock the required difficulty of every published competition song in advance. If a required chart is unavailable, up to ten minutes is allowed to unlock it. Failure to unlock it within that time results in disqualification.
- Competitors must arrive on time. Lateness or absence may lead to disqualification under the waiting procedure below.
- Competitors must consent to their nickname and gameplay being shown, recorded and made public through the competition livestream.
- Warm-up play is allowed in the spectator area. Spectators and other competitors must not interrupt those currently competing.

The arbiter checks the device, game version, headphones, chart availability and streaming consent before play. An operator's recorded checklist is a manual check, not an automatic inspection of a device or consent.

## Check-in and playing order

Competitors draw unique participant numbers at check-in. A number identifies the same competitor throughout the event and determines their qualifier playing order; it does not improve their ranking or score.

Arbiters call competitors approximately ten minutes before their match and bring them to the competition room. Qualifier entrants play in ascending participant-number order, in batches of up to three. After each batch, the arbiters record and check its results before bringing in the next entrants.

## Qualifiers

The number of registered competitors determines the format: **Plan A for 20 or fewer competitors**, or **Plan B for more than 20**. Six designated qualifier songs, divided into two distinct sets of three, are announced in advance. Each stage or group uses its own three-song scores; scores never carry over to a different stage or group.

### Plan A: first round and revival round

1. All competitors play the first set of three songs.
2. The top four advance directly to double elimination. They do not play the revival round.
3. The remaining eligible competitors play the different second set of three songs, starting with empty scores.
4. The top four from the revival round also advance, giving eight qualifiers in total.

The arbiter must confirm the first round's four qualifiers before the revival round begins. For seeding, the system's stated default places the first round's four qualifiers in seeds 1–4, in their first-round order, followed by the revival round's four in seeds 5–8, in their revival-round order. The proposal does not explicitly define the order between these two sets; ACA may record a different adjudicated seed order before bracket play begins.

### Plan B: two groups

Competitors are grouped by the parity of their drawn number. The system labels **odd-numbered competitors Group A** and **even-numbered competitors Group B**. The two groups play different sets of three songs, and the top four of each advance. Group A/B labels are operational labels; the proposal requires odd/even groups without consistently assigning those labels.

Do not compare the total scores of the two groups to establish a single ranking: they played different songs. The opening matches cross the groups as follows:

| Group A entrant | Group B opponent |
| --- | --- |
| A1 | B4 |
| A2 | B3 |
| A3 | B2 |
| A4 | B1 |

The system's cross-group seed order is A1, B1, A2, B2, A3, B3, A4, B4. It preserves the required opening opponents and provides a stated ordering for later highest-versus-lowest reseeding. ACA may review this order before bracket play begins.

### Qualifier scoring and ties

Scores are the **game's displayed score, including combo score**. Record all three scores and the displayed accuracy (ACC); also record Perfect, Good, Bad and Miss counts where available. Zero is a valid score. A blank field means no valid result has been entered and must not be treated as zero.

Rank competitors by their three-song total. When totals tie, compare accuracy first. For this event, use the equally weighted mean of the three original displayed ACC percentages, rounded to four decimal places. This aggregation was confirmed for the event because the proposal does not specify a multi-song method. Do not calculate Phigros scores from the old Project Sekai judgement formula.

If both total and ACC remain tied at a qualification boundary or at a seed position that must be resolved, the tied competitors play a randomly chosen tiebreaker from Phigros's full song list. Repeat as necessary until the required order is determined. Each tiebreaker is recorded separately; it does not change the original three-song total. The arbiter confirms its results before they affect qualification.

Once a qualifier tiebreak starts, scorekeepers may only enter the active extra-song results. A correction to a base score or its accuracy/judgements requires main-control review, a recorded reason and explicit confirmation that the round's tiebreaks will be invalidated. The old field and complete tiebreak records are archived for the arbiters before the order is recalculated. This is a record-correction procedure, not an additional rematch allowance.

For a software draw, the local song library must contain the available full-game songs from which the arbiter wishes to draw. Songs outside the ordinary double-elimination pool may be added to this library and used for qualifier tiebreaks. A small sample library is not a complete Phigros song list.

Do not advance a round with missing valid results or an unresolved qualifying tie. Disqualified competitors cannot qualify and do not require invented zero scores. Cases with fewer than four eligible qualifiers in a stage or group require an ACA decision about the remaining places.

## Double elimination

Eight seed slots enter a seeded Winners' Bracket (WB) and Losers' Bracket (LB). The scheduled format has **14 matches in six rounds**, including **one decisive Grand Final**. There is no bracket reset. Losing in the WB sends an eligible competitor to the LB; losing in the LB eliminates them.

The Grand Final is the stated exception to the general two-loss rule: its loser is the First Runner-up even if that competitor previously had no losses. Disqualification removes a competitor from the entire competition and does not give them another opportunity in the LB.

### Opening pairs and reseeding

For Plan A, the opening pairs are seed 1 versus 8, 2 versus 7, 3 versus 6, and 4 versus 5. Plan B uses the cross-group pairs above.

In each four-person R2 field, reseed its entrants by their original qualifier seed: the highest seed faces the lowest, and the two middle seeds face each other. Apply the same rule to the four-person R3 LB field. The complete upstream field must be known before these opponents are determined. This differs from a fixed bracket that always connects the same pair of previous match IDs.

| Round | Scheduled matches | Where competitors go |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R1 | Four WB matches | Winners enter R2 WB; losers enter R2 LB. |
| R2 | Two WB matches and two LB matches | WB winners enter the R3 WB Final; WB losers join the R3 LB field. LB winners join that same R3 LB field; LB losers are eliminated. |
| R3 | One WB Final and two LB matches | The WB Final winner enters R6 GF; its loser waits for the R5 LB Final. LB winners enter R4; LB losers are eliminated. |
| R4 | One LB match | The winner enters the R5 LB Final; the loser is eliminated. |
| R5 | One LB Final | The winner enters R6 GF; the loser finishes Third. |
| R6 | One Grand Final | The winner is Champion; the loser is First Runner-up. |

The proposal occasionally refers to GF as R5 or the LB Final as R4 in outgoing-route sentences. The six round headings and the fourteen-match schedule above resolve those numbering errors.

### Regular match song selection: R1–R5

1. Randomly draw six distinct songs from the announced double-elimination pool.
2. Each competitor may ban one candidate. A competitor may instead explicitly pass. A song already banned by the opponent cannot receive a second ban.
3. Once both ban/pass decisions are recorded, randomly draw two distinct songs from the remaining candidates.
4. Both competitors play both songs. The higher two-song game-score total wins, subject to the tie procedure below.

The candidate draw, ban/pass decisions and selected songs are recorded by the main operator. Final song selections lock the ban decisions.

### Grand Final: R6

Select **six distinct candidates**. These may include songs outside the ordinary double-elimination pool. The audience selects two songs by vote or through the MC's interpretation of audience reactions; the host selects a different third song. Both finalists play all three. The higher three-song total wins, subject to the tie procedure.

The software records the audience/MC selections; it does not operate a public voting service. If the host's selection is sealed in the broadcast, the main operator must reveal it before that third song is selected for play or scored. This broadcast reveal is an additional presentation feature, not a different competition rule.

### Match ties

For a tied regular-match or Grand-Final total, compare the displayed ACC first, using the equally weighted mean of the match's two or three songs under the confirmed event interpretation above. Missing ACC is missing evidence, not zero and not a resolved tie.

If the total and ACC remain equal, randomly select a tiebreaker from the announced double-elimination song pool. Record both competitors' valid results and have the arbiter confirm them. Repeat if the tiebreaker still does not determine a winner. Tiebreaker scores are recorded separately and do not replace or inflate the original match total.

The main operator must confirm that the arbiter has double-checked the scores before recording a result. Do not choose an arbitrary winner to bypass an unresolved tie. An exceptional ACA decision must have a recorded reason.

## Valid score evidence and rematches

Competitors must remain on the game's score screen after each song until an arbiter has recorded the result. All entered scores must be manually double-checked by the arbiters.

If a competitor leaves the score screen:

1. Check the YouTube livestream recording for the missing result.
2. If valid evidence is recovered, use that result.
3. If it cannot be recovered, a rematch is permitted only when the competitor states that leaving was unintentional and **all competitors in that match agree**.
4. Otherwise, the competitor is disqualified.

If a game error or glitch prevents the score screen from appearing, at most one rematch of that song is permitted.

The system applies **one shared rematch allowance**, whether the incident is a game error or leaving the score page. In qualifiers, the allowance belongs to the affected competitor, round and song; another competitor's separate incident does not consume it. In double elimination, both opponents share one allowance for that match song, and both replay it. This resolves the proposal's unspecified interaction between its two one-rematch clauses. Changing a scene, redrawing songs or resetting a match does not erase its rematch history. The arbiter records the cause, evidence checks, required agreement and decision before clearing an affected result. The system records a manual evidence check; it does not inspect YouTube or detect game errors automatically.

An unconfirmed tiebreak round can also receive one replay under the same evidence rules. Its allowance is independent of base songs; only that extra song's results clear, while base totals and earlier confirmed tiebreaks remain intact.

## Absence, chart problems and disqualification

For temporary absence or an unavailable required chart, an arbiter may begin a ten-minute wait. Record the competitor, reason and start time. If the competitor returns or unlocks the chart in time, cancel the wait. After the deadline, the arbiter confirms the disqualification; an elapsed software timer does not issue a penalty by itself.

- In qualifiers, the other entrants continue and the absent competitor is marked disqualified.
- In double elimination, the disqualified competitor's opponent advances without a fabricated played score.
- In a four-person field with exactly three eligible competitors and one vacancy, draw to determine which eligible competitor advances directly. The other two compete. Record the eligible entrants and the draw result before further play in that field.
- A disqualified competitor is excluded from future WB and LB matches. Keep their previous scores and the reason for the decision as historical records.
- If both opponents are unavailable, the field is smaller than specified, or a vacancy appears after part of the field has already been played, ACA must determine the appropriate outcome. Do not invent a result or silently redraw a completed field.

## Conduct and final authority

Competitors must respect other competitors and staff. If a rule breach does not affect the normal progress of the match or the opponent's scores, the proposal specifies no punishment. If it does, the rule breaker is disqualified. ACA may remove a person who disturbs, harasses or obstructs participants or staff. Helpers should prevent and resolve confrontation; a competitor who immediately remedies the issue may receive no punishment where the proposal allows it.

Damage to HKU or ACA property carries a minimum HK$100 charge. The responsible participant must also pay repair or replacement costs when they exceed HK$100. These venue matters are handled by the organisers, not by score calculations.

ACA has final authority over disputes, interpretations and decisions not covered by these rules. Record the reason for a software adjudication; preserve the original scores and evidence where possible. The main operator, not the scorekeeper, handles qualification confirmation, draws, result confirmation, waits, rematches, disqualification and other adjudications. Scorekeepers enter results for the active sheet only.

## Operator reminders

- Before play: verify the official roster, entrant numbers, qualifier plan, six announced qualifier songs, regular DE pool, game chart difficulties and the manually checked readiness information. The bundled players and song metadata are examples.
- Before moving on: save the current entrants' scores, ACC and available judgement counts; have the arbiter check them; resolve ties and confirm the round or match.
- Before a tiebreaker: add any required legitimate song metadata to the local library. A random software draw covers only the songs actually available there.
- Before showing competitors: inspect the actual OBS capture-card and handcam feeds. HTML assignments and readiness records do not verify a physical video source.
- Use the live bracket's actual seed context for advancement routes. An older fixed-route fourteen-match diagram must be updated for the proposal's highest-versus-lowest reseeding; it cannot predetermine every R2/R3 opponent.
- Keep the full-score screenshots or recording evidence available to arbiters. A broadcast total is not a substitute for the underlying results.
- Existing event files may retain the former fixed-bracket/two-group rules and eight-candidate Grand Finals. Configure the proposal format explicitly for a new event; do not reinterpret completed historical results during an upgrade.
- Confirm any unresolved interpretation with ACA before competition day. The linked audit identifies the stated operational defaults and the proposal's inconsistencies.
